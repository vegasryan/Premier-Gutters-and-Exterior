What fascia and soffit are

Look up at the edge of your roof: the board facing out is the fascia, and the panel under the overhang is the soffit.

Fascia
It runs the length of the roof’s edge, over the ends of the rafters. Your gutters hang on it.
Soffit
It covers the underside of the overhang, between the fascia and the wall. Many have vents that let the attic breathe.
Why they matter
In good shape, they keep water out of the wood at your roof’s edge, and birds and pests out of the attic.

Signs your fascia needs repair

Your gutters hang on the fascia board, so trouble with one often shows in the other. Here’s where to look.

Wood rot and decay

Check the wood under your roof edge. If it’s soft, spongy or crumbly, water has gotten in.

Gutters sagging or pulling away

Hangers hold your gutter to the board. When the wood weakens, they let go.

Peeling or blistering paint

Look along the face of the board. Bubbling or flaking paint means water is trapped behind it.

Cracking and warping

Check the corners and the long runs in full sun. Sun and swings in temperature crack and warp boards.

Hail dents

After a storm, look at the face of your gutters and the metal wrap on the board.

Fascia and the jobs around it

Fascia is rarely a job on its own. It often gets replaced while new gutters go up, or after a hailstorm hits the roof edge. Here’s how we handle each, with a link to the page that covers it.

Materials

We repair and replace the fascia behind your gutters and the soffit under your eaves, in one of four materials.

Aluminum

A wood board covered in bent aluminum, so rain and sun hit the metal instead of the wood, and there’s no wood left to paint.

Vinyl

PVC

A solid PVC board that replaces wood fascia outright. It won’t rot, and a groove in its back holds the edge of the soffit.
